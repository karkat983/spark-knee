# Sized datasets

Built by `python -m src.make_sizes` into `data/sized/size=<N>x/` (gitignored); the table is
`results/sizes.csv`, regenerated by `python -m src.size_report`. 1x = January 2024
(docs/decisions/001-size-unit.md). Every file holds ~1/4 of a month.

| Size | Rows | Rows vs 1x | Files | Parquet MB | Made of |
|------|------|-----------|-------|------------|---------|
| 0.25x | 742,483 | 0.25 | 1 | 15.1 | 25% sample of Jan (seed 42) |
| 0.5x | 1,482,533 | 0.50 | 2 | 34.5 | 50% sample of Jan (seed 42) |
| 1x | 2,964,624 | 1.00 | 4 | 69.0 | Jan |
| 2x | 5,972,150 | 2.01 | 8 | 139.4 | Jan-Feb |
| 4x | 13,069,067 | 4.41 | 16 | 305.2 | Jan-Apr |
| 8x | 26,304,243 | 8.87 | 32 | 615.5 | Jan-Jun + Jan', Feb' (shifted 364 days) |
| 16x | 53,733,253 | 18.13 | 64 | 1,259.6 | Jan-Jun + Jan'-Jun' + Jan''-Apr'' |

**Rows, not multiples, are the size axis.** Spring months carry up to 26% more trips than
January, so "16x" is really 18.1x January's rows. Every analysis plots runtime against rows.

The re-written files are ~1.4x larger per row than TLC's originals (69 MB vs 50 MB for January):
`repartition` deals rows out round-robin, which breaks the time ordering that made the original
columns compress well. Read cost per row is therefore slightly higher than for the raw files, the
same at every size.

## Skewed variant

`python -m src.make_sizes --skew` also writes `data/sized_skew/size=<N>x/`: the same data plus
shifted copies of every trip picked up at zone 132 (JFK Airport, the busiest zone at 4.9% of
January trips) until that one zone holds half of all rows.

| Size | Rows | Zone 132 rows | Zone 132 share |
|------|------|---------------|----------------|
| 1x skewed | 5,724,184 | 2,904,800 | 50.75% |
| 4x skewed | 25,411,754 | 12,930,434 | 50.88% |

19 extra copies of the hot zone are needed (`copies_for_share`). The skewed sets are about twice
the rows of their unskewed sizes, so they are compared with unskewed runs at equal row counts,
not equal labels.
